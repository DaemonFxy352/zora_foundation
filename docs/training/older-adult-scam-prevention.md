# DRAFT — Older adult scam prevention

Audience: Older adults and trusted community supporters

Suggested duration: 60 minutes.

## Learning objectives

- Identify pressure and impersonation without relying on caller ID or a familiar voice.
- Demonstrate an independent callback using a fictional request.
- Choose the affected bank or service as an immediate response destination after a suspected scam.

## Facilitator preparation

- Review all linked guidance and reporting destinations before use; confirm a named facilitator and host contact.
- Agree on safeguarding, escalation, accessibility, and privacy responsibilities with the host. Do not collect account details or personal incident evidence.
- Print the linked draft handouts only for review until approved. Explain that examples are fictional and participants may pass on any activity.

## Session outline

### Welcome and choice — 5 minutes

Explain scope, privacy, and the option to pass. Ask participants to name a task they want more confidence doing without describing past losses.

Discussion prompt: What would make this session comfortable for you?

### Recognize pressure — 10 minutes

Read a fictional message claiming a bank needs a transfer. Identify the requested decision rather than judging the participant.

Discussion prompt: What is being requested, and what could wait?

### Phone, message, and AI impersonation — 10 minutes

Discuss why an official-looking number or familiar-sounding voice does not establish identity. Do not play cloned voices or stage a surprise call.

Discussion prompt: What can you check independently of what you hear?

### Practice verification — 15 minutes

On paper, work through a fictional family money request. Choose a known callback route and a second contact; no actual numbers or transfers.

Discussion prompt: If the first trusted person cannot answer, what is your next safe step?

### Respond without further loss — 10 minutes

Use the immediate-action handout to choose bank, account, or device help for three fictional situations.

Discussion prompt: Which service can act on the affected payment or account?

### Takeaway and feedback — 10 minutes

Invite each participant to explain one step in their own words. Collect optional feedback about clarity, not personal incidents.

Discussion prompt: What will you practice next, and what support would help?

## Participant takeaways

- An independent callback is a normal safety step, not an accusation.
- No one needs to share passwords with a helper.
- Act promptly after suspected fraud without promises of recovery.

## Accessibility and adaptation

- Offer large print, read-aloud or text alternatives, clear audio, seated participation, and breaks.
- Allow spoken, written, or pointing responses. No personal device, login, payment, or public disclosure is required.
- Ask about language and access needs in advance; arrange interpretation or adaptation with the host rather than improvising sensitive guidance.
