Audience: Parents, guardians, caregivers, and family-serving organizations

Suggested duration: 60 minutes.

## Learning objectives

- Create an age-adapted help-seeking conversation.
- Identify privacy, contact, and purchase settings to review together.
- Explain an independent family verification step and appropriate reporting route.

## Facilitator preparation

- Review all linked guidance and reporting destinations before use; confirm a named facilitator and host contact.
- Agree on safeguarding, escalation, accessibility, and privacy responsibilities with the host. Do not collect account details or personal incident evidence.
- Print the linked draft handouts only for review until approved. Explain that examples are fictional and participants may pass on any activity.

## Session outline

### Set a supportive tone — 5 minutes

Explain that families have different needs and may pass. Avoid comparing parenting decisions.

Discussion prompt: What would help a child feel able to ask for help?

### Age-appropriate privacy — 10 minutes

Compare a fictional young child profile with a fictional teen profile. Choose which details should remain private without viewing real accounts.

Discussion prompt: How would your explanation change with the child’s understanding?

### Gaming and messaging — 10 minutes

Discuss an invented prize message in plain text. Do not create a functioning link or ask participants to simulate coercion.

Discussion prompt: What access or information does the request ask for?

### AI and family verification — 10 minutes

Explain that synthetic media cannot prove identity. Choose a known callback route for an imagined family request.

Discussion prompt: How can adults model pausing rather than demanding instant decisions?

### Practice a supportive response — 15 minutes

In pairs or individually, complete the parent conversation guide. Practice the adult’s calm response to a child asking for help, with no exploitation role-play.

Discussion prompt: What could you say before asking detailed questions?

### Reporting and next steps — 10 minutes

Review platform reporting, trusted adults, and NCMEC resources. Each participant chooses one household setting or conversation to revisit.

Discussion prompt: Who can help when an issue is beyond a household rule?

## Participant takeaways

- Settings support ongoing conversations; they do not replace them.
- Help comes before blame or a discussion of broken rules.
- Never forward intimate images to seek advice.

## Accessibility and adaptation

- Offer large print, read-aloud or text alternatives, clear audio, seated participation, and breaks.
- Allow spoken, written, or pointing responses. No personal device, login, payment, or public disclosure is required.
- Ask about language and access needs in advance; arrange interpretation or adaptation with the host rather than improvising sensitive guidance.
