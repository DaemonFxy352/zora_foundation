Audience: Teenagers, with a prepared adult facilitator and host safeguarding contact

Suggested duration: 45–60 minutes.

## Learning objectives

- Identify an account or social pressure request that deserves a pause.
- Explain why images, voices, and account names do not by themselves prove identity.
- Find a trusted adult and appropriate reporting support without needing to disclose personal harm.

## Facilitator preparation

- Review all linked guidance and reporting destinations before use; confirm a named facilitator and host contact.
- Agree on safeguarding, escalation, accessibility, and privacy responsibilities with the host. Do not collect account details or personal incident evidence.
- Print the linked draft handouts only for review until approved. Explain that examples are fictional and participants may pass on any activity.

## Session outline

### Choice and boundaries — 5 minutes

Explain privacy, the right to pass, and where to seek private help. Do not promise confidentiality that conflicts with safeguarding responsibilities.

Discussion prompt: What makes asking for help easier?

### Identity and privacy — 10 minutes

Use a fictional profile and ask what strangers could learn. Never open students’ accounts or ask for identifying information.

Discussion prompt: Which details would you choose not to share publicly?

### Scams and AI-generated content — 10 minutes

Use a benign text example asking for a game-account code. Discuss social pressure and independent checking; no deepfake generation or exploitation scenarios.

Discussion prompt: What action matters more than whether a message looks convincing?

### Boundaries and safe help-seeking — 10 minutes

Give a short non-graphic explanation of sextortion as threats involving intimate images. State that it is not the person’s fault, that payment/more images are not a safe solution, and point to trusted adults and NCMEC. No role-play, images, or disclosure prompts.

Discussion prompt: Which adult or support route could someone approach without explaining it to the group?

### Practice and takeaways — 10 minutes

Use the teen checklist with fictional account settings. Invite a voluntary explanation of one safety choice. Offer private written questions without names.

Discussion prompt: What would you tell a friend who needs help?

Optional 15 minutes: repeat the benign account-code exercise using a different fictional request, allow a break, and answer general questions. Do not extend the sensitive-content discussion through personal examples.

## Participant takeaways

- You can leave a conversation even if someone pressures you.
- A trusted adult can help with reporting and account recovery.
- Do not pay blackmail demands or forward intimate images; seek support.

## Accessibility and adaptation

- Offer large print, read-aloud or text alternatives, clear audio, seated participation, and breaks.
- Allow spoken, written, or pointing responses. No personal device, login, payment, or public disclosure is required.
- Ask about language and access needs in advance; arrange interpretation or adaptation with the host rather than improvising sensitive guidance.
