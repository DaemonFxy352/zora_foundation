## F1 — Payment-provider response and transfer recall

**Current wording — [What to do after a scam](https://zora-foundation-poeofm04v-zora-safe.vercel.app/education/after-a-scam), `/education/after-a-scam`:**

> Card or bank transfer: call your card issuer, bank or credit union. Payment app: report through the app’s genuine support. Wire-transfer service: contact that service. Say you were deceived, identify the transaction and ask whether it can be stopped, reversed or refunded. Explain whether you made the payment after being deceived or someone else made it; ask which dispute process applies.

**Current wording — [What to do after a scam](https://zora-foundation-poeofm04v-zora-safe.vercel.app/education/after-a-scam), `/education/after-a-scam`:**

> Gift card: contact its issuer and keep the card and receipt. Cryptocurrency: contact the exchange or ATM operator used; recovery may be difficult. Cash sent by delivery: contact the carrier promptly to ask whether an undelivered package can be intercepted. Options depend on the method and circumstances; no refund is assured.

**Current wording — [Payment redirection scams: verify changed payment details](https://zora-foundation-poeofm04v-zora-safe.vercel.app/education/payment-redirection), `/education/payment-redirection`:**

> If a transfer was sent, contact your financial institution immediately, identify the transfer and ask it to contact the receiving institution about stopping or recalling it. Do not wait to complete a crime report. Notify the relevant organizational support team, preserve the instructions and transaction identifiers privately, and report internet-enabled fraud to the FBI’s Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3), linked below. Reporting does not guarantee an investigation or return of funds.

**Decision required:** Are the bank/card/app/wire/gift-card/crypto/carrier distinctions and bank-to-bank recall sequence accurate and useful? Confirm that describing who made a payment does not imply a legal determination about reimbursement rights.

**Recommended correction/disposition (not implemented):** Retain provider-first urgency and factual transaction descriptions. No refund entitlement or deadline should be added without applicable authority. If the specialist identifies a method-specific omission, supply a concise correction and jurisdiction rather than a universal recovery rule.

**Primary references:** [consumer.ftc.gov guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed); [www.fbi.gov guidance](https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/common-frauds-and-scams/business-email-compromise). ## F2 — Independent bank and family callbacks

**Current wording — [Recognize a scam](https://zora-foundation-poeofm04v-zora-safe.vercel.app/education/recognize-a-scam), `/education/recognize-a-scam`:**

> For a bank call, hang up and use the number on your card. For a text or email about an account, open your usual app yourself. Ask about the exact request before paying or sharing information.

**Current wording — [How to handle a phone impersonation scam](https://zora-foundation-poeofm04v-zora-safe.vercel.app/education/phone-impersonation), `/education/phone-impersonation`:**

> End the original call completely. Find a number on your bank card, statement, established contact list or the organization’s genuine site. Start a new call yourself; do not accept a transfer from the suspicious caller as verification.

**Current wording — [Family emergency scams: pause and check](https://zora-foundation-poeofm04v-zora-safe.vercel.app/education/family-emergency-scams), `/education/family-emergency-scams`:**

> A private family phrase can be an additional check. Keep it out of public posts and shared practice worksheets, and change it if exposed. Treat it as one layer, not a guarantee: knowing a phrase does not replace confirming the situation through a contact you trust.

**Decision required:** Can readers follow the established-number callback without trusting a caller-supplied number, transfer or phrase? Review accessibility where the first contact is unreachable; emergency safety is handled in C6, not delayed by payment verification.

**Recommended correction/disposition (not implemented):** Retain card/statement/saved-contact routes and the phrase-as-one-layer limitation. Keep genuine emergency response separate. No extra technical telephone timing instruction is proposed without a supporting source.

**Primary references:** [consumer.ftc.gov guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/phone-scams); [consumer.ftc.gov guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/scammers-use-fake-emergencies-steal-your-money); [www.ic3.gov guidance](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA241203). ## F4 — Account takeover, MFA and practical recovery access

**Current wording — [Make your accounts safer](https://zora-foundation-poeofm04v-zora-safe.vercel.app/education/account-safety), `/education/account-safety`:**

> Turn on multifactor authentication, sometimes called two-step verification. It adds a check beyond your password. Open the service’s security settings and follow its setup instructions. If offered, choose an authenticator app or security key; text codes are still useful when that is the only option. Never give another person a sign-in code or approve a login you did not start.

**Current wording — [Make your accounts safer](https://zora-foundation-poeofm04v-zora-safe.vercel.app/education/account-safety), `/education/account-safety`:**

> If you lose access, use the provider’s official recovery process. After regaining control, sign out other sessions and check recovery settings. In email settings, remove forwarding rules you did not create so new messages do not keep going to someone else. Warn contacts if your account sent messages in your name. A stranger promising instant recovery is not the provider’s support team.

**Current wording — [Online safety for older adults: a practical guide](https://zora-foundation-poeofm04v-zora-safe.vercel.app/education/online-safety-older-adults), `/education/online-safety-older-adults`:**

> End the interaction and contact the affected bank or service directly. If remote access was granted, use another trusted device for sensitive account changes and seek help from a support provider you chose. If you cannot use another device, call your bank through a known number or visit a branch to ask about protecting the account while the device is checked. Keep transaction details and ask the payment provider about recovery options; repayment is not guaranteed.

**Decision required:** Are security-settings instructions, SMS-only fallback, session/forwarding-rule review and private recovery codes clear without implying all MFA methods resist phishing? Does the second-device/phone/branch alternative work for readers with limited access?

**Recommended correction/disposition (not implemented):** Retain the directly supported FTC comparison and provider-led recovery. If a provider lacks a branch or an accessible second-device option, recommend an available official phone/support route rather than a universal branch promise. CISA fetch denial is a verification limit, not evidence against the FTC guidance.

**Primary references:** [consumer.ftc.gov guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/use-two-factor-authentication-protect-your-accounts); [consumer.ftc.gov guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recover-your-hacked-email-or-social-media-account); [consumer.ftc.gov guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-spot-avoid-and-report-tech-support-scams).
